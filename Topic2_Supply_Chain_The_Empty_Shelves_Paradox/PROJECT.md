# Topic 2. Supply Chain (Brookshire’s / Walmart): The Empty Shelves Paradox

## The problem

A grocery chain’s biggest sales days are quietly losing it money. Brookshire’s point-of-sale data shows a pattern nobody expected: on the days it runs its biggest discount promotions, profit margins collapse and shelves go empty for items that are not even on sale. Management’s fix is simple: ban promotions entirely. That would change how a regional grocer competes with Walmart. Find out whether promotions are really the problem before they are gone.

## Inputs from the people who run the chain

1. **Pricing manager:** A promotion is a temporary price cut that lasts one ad week. Every zone runs a weekly ad. A few times a quarter, most often around holidays, a zone runs a big sale event instead, with deeper cuts on many more products.
2. **Marketing director:** A big sale brings more shoppers through the doors. Some of them buy only the advertised products.
3. **IT (point of sale):** The register records each item scanned at checkout.
4. **Store operations:** Stock is counted every morning before the doors open. Deliveries from the distribution center arrive overnight, on a fixed schedule for each department. Fresh departments get a truck most nights. The other departments get one two or three times a week.
5. **Distribution center manager:** A truck holds only so much. In an ad week, the advertised products are loaded first.
6. **Finance:** Margin is price minus unit cost. The margin of a product on a day is the units sold times (price minus unit cost).
