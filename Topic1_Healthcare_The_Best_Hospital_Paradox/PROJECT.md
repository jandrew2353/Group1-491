# Topic 1. Healthcare: The "Best" Hospital Paradox

## The problem

An algorithm is about to defund the hospitals that treat the sickest patients. The region’s top research hospitals have a much higher patient mortality rate than small rural clinics running on a fraction of the budget. An insurance algorithm has done the math and recommends cutting the research hospitals’ funding. On paper, it saves money and lives. Before anyone signs off, prove whether the data is telling the truth.

## Inputs from the hospital administrators

1. Patients who are sicker when they arrive are more likely to die in hospital. Death rates also differ widely by diagnosis and by age.
2. Every network hospital scores each patient's acuity in the first hours after arrival, from vital signs and laboratory results.
3. Research hospitals have the specialists, operating rooms and intensive care beds that smaller hospitals lack, and smaller hospitals send them patients they cannot treat.
4. Before a transfer, the sending hospital stabilises the patient for the trip.
5. A transferred patient has one record at each hospital: the sending hospital closes its stay as a transfer and the receiving hospital opens a new stay.
6. A death is recorded on the stay at the hospital where it happens.
