# Topic 5. Education: The Tutoring Paradox

## The problem

A school offered extra help. The students who took it failed more often. The school launched a free tutoring program to improve pass rates. At the end of the year, students who attended tutoring were more likely to fail than students who did not. A basic algorithm points straight at the program, and administrators are ready to cut its funding. Before they cancel it, find out what is really driving the failures.

## Inputs from the district

The program coordinator, teachers and the district's records office told us:
1. Tutoring is free and voluntary. Teachers and counselors refer the students they are concerned about; parents and students can also ask for a place.
2. A referred student is given a fixed weekly 45-minute session with a tutor at his or her own school, in groups of up to four, until the end of the school year.
3. Tutors record at every scheduled session whether the student was present.
4. Every course is graded at the end of each term, and each term is graded on its own. A term grade below 60 percent (an F) is a failing grade.
5. Each student's grade point average from the previous school year is kept on the student record.
6. Tutoring takes place after the last bell, and the district runs no late buses.
